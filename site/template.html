    02 / From papers

    Questions the papers themselves leave open

    Everything above has stood for decades — which is what makes it a poor place to start. These are the smaller questions authors state in their own conclusions: a gap they could not close, an assumption they could not drop, a case they did not handle. Every one is quoted from the paper, and a quote that cannot be found in the source text is discarded rather than shown, so nothing here is a question a model invented.

    03 / Resolved

    Taken off the list

    Problems that were settled, kept here with the paper that did it. They are off the open list, but throwing the entry away would throw away the most useful part — what it took, and how long the gap between the conjecture and the proof actually was.

    Data sources

    All conference metadata was fetched from DBLP's official search API, covering every proceedings of FOCS, STOC, SODA, ITCS (conf/innovations in DBLP) and EC (conf/sigecom), crawled in August 2026. The daily tab comes from arXiv's Atom API instead, taking everything first submitted that day to cs.CC, cs.DS, cs.DM, cs.GT and cs.CG.

    DBLP carries no abstracts, so those come from OpenAlex, matched by DOI where one exists and otherwise by exact title and year. About — of papers have one; the rest show no abstract toggle at all. Abstracts load on demand, so the browser fetches them only for papers you open.

    Where the topic labels come from

    Hand-written regular expressions matched against paper titles; one paper can match several topics. This is a rough heuristic classification, not a semantic model: papers whose titles omit the keywords are missed, and ambiguous words produce false matches. About a quarter of all papers fall under no topic at all, and that share is much higher in the early decades. Read the trends as orders of magnitude, not as precise measurements.

    How the daily tab is built

    Three layers, each of which degrades on its own. arXiv's API supplies the day's new submissions; keyword rules assign topics and a score; and only the top fifteen go to a model, which writes the result, method and significance lines from the abstract alone — it has not read the paper.

    So it can misread, restate an author's claim as established fact, or garble a formula, and the star rating is its opinion, not peer review. These are preprints: nothing here has been refereed. Treat the tab as a filter and open what interests you on arXiv.

    How the open-problem list is maintained

    The problems and their evidence are written by hand and kept in the repository, so every claim about the state of the art is a person's, not a model's. What the pipeline adds is signals: it scans the daily archive — and, on request, arXiv itself — for preprints whose abstracts match a problem and claim to move it, then has a model classify each one from the abstract alone.

    Nothing is ever marked resolved automatically. Preprints claiming proofs of famous conjectures appear every year and most are wrong, so a model's "resolves" verdict only files the problem under review; it takes a person reading the paper, and an edit to the list with a citation attached, for anything to move to Resolved.

    Known caveats

    Paper counts follow DBLP entries, with the proceedings' own editorship and its front-matter and table-of-contents entries removed — though some one-page abstracts remain, most noticeably at EC. Authors are deduplicated by DBLP author ID, so distinct people who share a name are not wrongly merged.
